Introduction & Objectives

Introduction

The purpose of the Disaster Management Manual is to provide step-by-step guidelines to all stakeholders responsible for dealing with different types of incidents, accidents, disasters, and emergencies.

Key Objectives

  • Save lives and alleviate suffering.
  • Provide help to stranded passengers and arrange their prompt evacuation.
  • Instill a sense of security amongst all concerned by providing accurate information.
  • Protect Metro Rail property.
  • Ascertain the cause of the accident.
  • Expedite the restoration of train operations.

Serious Incidents & Classifications

Recognized Serious Incidents

  • Fire, smoke emission, or explosion in metro trains/premises.
  • Derailment of a train.
  • Collision of trains.
  • Security threats, terrorist attacks, or bomb explosions.
  • Chemical or biological gas release in trains or stations.
  • Natural calamities: cyclones, floods, and earthquakes.

Operational Definitions

Incident: Any event which causes or has the potential to cause delay or disruption to train services.

Accident: Any event which has the potential to or does actually cause loss of life, injury, and/or damage to property.

Emergency / Disaster: Events that may or may not necessarily be the outcome of train accidents, but have the potential to cause loss of life or injury to human beings.

The OCS shall:

  • Ensure the safety of passengers and personnel at the site.
  • Ascertain that all injured and stranded passengers have been safely evacuated.
  • Ensure that all personnel at the site work efficiently.

Fire Safety & Extinguishing Protocols

Fire is one of the most critical hazards in metro operations. Key threat areas include:

Electrical Equipment Fire
Train Fire
Station Premises Fire
⚠️ CRITICAL SAFETY RULE:
No water shall EVER be used to extinguish an electrical fire.

Immediate Fire Actions

  • Power De-energization: Power supply must be cut off immediately by informing the Traction Power Controller.
  • Suppression Methods: Use inert gas flooding and clean-agent extinguishers instead of water for electrical systems.

FIRE IN A METRO TRAIN

Subtitle: Emergency Response and Duties of Operating Staff

Duties of the Train Operator (TO)

  • Initial Alert & Assessment: Immediately inform the Operational Control Centre (OCC) and assess the fire to decide if the train can safely reach the next station.
  • Onboard Crowd Control: Move passengers away from affected cars into safer coaches; request traveling Metro staff to assist in relief work.
  • Transit to Station (If Safe): Drive to the next station while informing OCC and using the PA system to tell passengers not to panic and prepare to vacate. Give evacuation priority to the aged, handicapped, women, and children.
  • Disabled Train Protocol (If Unsafe): If unsafe to proceed or disabled, advise OCC, switch mode selector to 'OFF', and trip all circuit breakers.
  • Firefighting & Evacuation: Use onboard fire extinguishers. If extinguished, proceed under OCC ATC instructions; if uncontrolled, immediately prepare passenger evacuation.
  • Platform Evacuation: If at a platform, open all doors on the platform side, instruct passengers to vacate, inform OCC/Station Controller, and seek station staff help.

FIRE IN A METRO TRAIN

Subtitle: Emergency Response and Duties of Operating Staff

Duties of the Traffic Controller (TC) / Chief Controller (CC)

  • Agency Mobilization: Inform internal/external agencies and adjoining station staff to rush to the site (or adjacent stations if mid-section) for evacuation support.
  • Line Protection: If stopped mid-section, stop all train movement on adjacent tracks immediately.
  • Station Clearing: Vacate waiting passengers from adjacent stations to assist evacuees and facilitate emergency vehicle/medical movement.
  • Coordination & Communication: Ask CC to alert the Disaster Management Team (DMT) and required departments. Expedite medical/fire services, regulate train services, and keep stations informed of delays.

FIRE IN A METRO STATION

Subtitle: Multi-Agency Coordination and Duties of Station Personnel

Duties of the Station Controller / Manager (SC/SM)

  • Alert & PA Evacuation: Inform fire personnel and OCC. Announce via PA to vacate the platform using earmarked exit routes.
  • Crowd & Access Control: Inform security/police to maintain order and control crowds. Stop ticket booking and sales immediately.
  • Containment: Close station entry and platform exits near the fire to prevent access. If platform exits are blocked, prevent access and disperse existing passengers.
  • Fire Suppression & Power: Use fire extinguishers and suppression systems with staff help. Advise electrical staff to cut power to the affected area.

FIRE IN A METRO STATION

Subtitle: Multi-Agency Coordination and Duties of Station Personnel

Duties of the Traffic Controller (TC)

  • Emergency Notification: Alert fire services, medical teams, DMT, and the Traction Power Controller (TPC) regarding electrical safety.
  • Service Regulation & PA: Regulate train services on both sides of the station and inform all affected stations of delays. Make regular system-wide announcements explaining train regulations.
  • Platform Bypass Protocol: If tracks are safe, advise approaching Train Operators to instruct passengers not to detrain. Open train doors exclusively to evacuate waiting passengers from the platform.

Duties of the Traction Power Controller (TPC)

  • Power De-energization: Instruct station electrical staff to switch off power specifically in the fire-affected station area.
  • Firefighting Support: Advise maintenance staff to fight the fire using available equipment.

Duties of the Security Controller

  • Police & Fire Coordination: Coordinate with OCC, alert SSF/Local Police, and request Delhi Fire Services assistance.
  • Staff Deployment: Deploy security staff to assist with evacuation, crowd control, and active firefighting.

TRAIN COLLISION

Subtitle: Emergency Reporting Protocols and Multi-Department Duties

System Overview & Initial Reporting Protocol

  • Automatic Protection: The Automatic Train Protection (ATP) system ensures train separation and zero collision during normal operations.
  • Degraded Mode Vigilance: Because ATP protection is not available in Restricted Operational Mode (ROS), Restricted Manual (RM), and Cut Out Modes, Train Operators working in degraded modes must be extra vigilant to ensure safe train movement.
  • Witness Reporting: Any employee witnessing, discovering, or being involved in a train collision must immediately inform the Operational Control Centre (OCC) and provide the following vital details:
    • Caller's name and identification.
    • Reason for the call and train identification.
    • Exact location of the collision.
    • Whether medical assistance is required.
    • The presence of any smoke or fire.

TRAIN COLLISION

Subtitle: Emergency Reporting Protocols and Multi-Department Duties

Duties of the Train Operator (TO)

  • Immediate Alert: In the event of a collision involving their train, the TO must immediately inform the OCC and give as many details as possible.
  • Track Protection: If the adjacent track is infringed, the TO's first and foremost duty is to protect the adjacent track to avoid multiple accidents.
  • Passenger Communication: Inform passengers about the incident via the public address system, advising them about the rescue and relief arrangements being made.
  • Injury Assessment & First Aid: Quickly assess the situation regarding passenger injuries, give the OCC as much detail as available, render first aid to passengers, and check for damage to the train.
  • Evacuation Protocol: Seek the OCC's permission before initiating any passenger evacuation, and strictly act as per the instructions of the OCC.

TRAIN COLLISION

Subtitle: Emergency Reporting Protocols and Multi-Department Duties

Duties of the Traffic Controller (TC)

  • Site Protection: Immediately block all train movements on both tracks to protect the accident site.
  • Emergency Mobilization: Inform the Disaster Management Team (DMT) and other designated departments and personnel, and mobilize medical assistance as required.
  • Relief Train Deployment: Inform the train depot of the concerned line to be in immediate readiness to move the Rescue Relief Train.
  • Staff & Service Regulation: Instruct station managers to depute station staff for passenger evacuation and medical aid. Regulate train services and inform all stations on the route regarding service effects and delays.
  • Environmental & Traffic Control: Activate the tunnel ventilation system if the collision is in an underground section, arrange for PA announcements to be made on trains and at stations, and initiate operating procedures to relieve train congestion at the collision site.

Duties of the Station Controller (SC)

  • OCC & Agency Alert: Immediately inform the OCC, arrange for immediate medical assistance, and inform the DMRP/Local Police.
  • Resource Mobilization: Mobilize station staff for the rapid evacuation of passengers and the rendering of first aid to the injured.
  • Crowd Information: Continuously inform passengers awaiting at the station of likely service delays.

TRAIN COLLISION

Subtitle: Emergency Reporting Protocols and Multi-Department Duties

Duties of the Traction Power Controller (CTPC/TPC)

  • Power De-energization: Normally, no power supply switching OFF decision shall be taken independently by the CTPC/TPC; however, if instructed by the Traffic Controller, the OHE/33kV power supply shall be switched OFF on the respective lines.
  • Field Communication: Convey the emergency message to senior officers and field staff as per the situation.
  • Restoration Dispatch: Instruct OHE staff to reach the accident site by the quickest available means to provide discharge rods so that site restoration work can commence safely.
  • Information Logging: Collect and report vital information to senior officers, including the caller's ID, train ID, collision location/mast number, adjacent line obstruction status, required medical aid, and presence of fire or smoke.

TRAIN DERAILMENT

Subtitle: Emergency Response and Duties of Operating Staff

Duties of the Train Operator (TO)

  • Track Protection: The TO's first and foremost duty is to protect the adjacent track as per authorized instructions to avoid a secondary accident.
  • Train Security & PA Communication: Secure the train immediately. Inform passengers of the problem and the rescue/relief actions being taken via the public address system.
  • Initial Alert & Information: Immediately inform the Operational Control Centre (OCC) and provide the following vital details:
    • Train Operator ID and exact train location.
    • Train description and current condition.
    • Whether the adjacent track is obstructed or clear.
    • Passenger injury status and the presence of any smoke or fire.
  • Evacuation Protocol: Seek specific instructions from the OCC before initiating any passenger evacuation.

TRAIN DERAILMENT

Subtitle: Multi-Agency Coordination and Control Room Duties

Duties of the Traffic Controller (TC)

  • Immediate Train Halt: Instruct Train Operators of all trains approaching the derailment site on both tracks to stop their trains immediately and report their positions.
  • Agency & Staff Mobilization: Notify the Disaster Management Team (DMT), all concerned departments, Metro Police, security personnel, and station managers on the affected line so they can inform waiting passengers of likely delays.
  • Medical & Relief Deployment: Mobilize medical assistance immediately and inform the train depot of the concerned line to be in readiness to move the Rescue Relief Train.
  • Evacuation & Service Regulation: Instruct station managers to depute station staff for passenger evacuation and medical aid. Regulate train services and inform all stations on the route regarding service impacts and delays.
  • Environmental & Traffic Control: Activate the tunnel ventilation system if the derailment occurred in an underground section, arrange for PA announcements to be made on trains and at stations, and initiate operating procedures to relieve train congestion at the site.

TRAIN DERAILMENT

Subtitle: Electrical Safety and Site Restoration Protocols

Duties of the Traction Power Controller (CTPC/TPC)

  • Power De-energization: Normally, no power supply switching OFF decision shall be taken independently by the CTPC/TPC; however, if instructed by the Traffic Controller, the OHE/33kV power supply shall be switched OFF on the respective lines.
  • Field Communication: Convey the emergency message to senior officers and field staff as per the unfolding situation.
  • Restoration Dispatch: Instruct OHE staff to reach the accident site by the quickest available means to provide discharge rods so that site restoration work can commence safely.
  • Information Logging: Collect and report vital information to senior officers, including the caller's name and ID, train ID, exact location/mast number, adjacent line obstruction status, required medical aid, and presence of fire or smoke.

STAMPEDE

Definition & Causes

  • Definition: A stampede is a sudden rush of a crowd of panic-stricken people. It may result in injury to persons, loss of human life, or extensive damage to the metro system.
  • Primary Causes:
    • Fire or serious accident inside the metro system.
    • Unusual rush during festivals.
    • Unusual rush of passengers after sporting events at stadiums, public demonstrations, or political rallies.
    • Natural calamities like heavy rains or earthquakes.
    • Heavy traffic on the road leading people to take the metro system.

STAMPEDE

Subtitle: Risk Overview and Immediate Station Crowd Control Protocols

System Overview & Immediate Crowd Control

  • High-Risk Locations & Causes: Stampedes are manmade disasters that can occur at busy interchanges (e.g., Rajiv Chowk, Kashmere Gate) or very deep stations (e.g., Chawri Bazar) due to excessive passenger overcrowding, bomb blasts/terrorist attacks, or panic-inducing rumors.
  • Emergency Evacuation Protocol: If caused by a terrorist attack in the metro premises, the station must be evacuated immediately by opening the flaps of all Automatic Fare Collection (AFC) gates as well as emergency exits.
  • Service Disruption Crowd Control: If caused by a disruption in normal train frequency due to technical or other reasons, the following immediate actions must be taken:
    • Make immediate efforts to evacuate the station premises.
    • Close station entry gates immediately to stop the fresh entry of rushing commuters.
    • Make regular announcements via the PA system informing passengers about the real nature of the problem.
    • Deploy clear signage at entry points to divert approaching commuters.
    • Position NMRC and security staff at entrance points to provide accurate information and prevent rumors.

STAMPEDE

Subtitle: Operating Staff and Control Room Emergency Duties

Duties of the Train Operator (TO)

  • Immediate Halt: Stop the train forthwith and immediately inform the Operational Control Centre (OCC) for further directions.
  • Passenger Communication: Announce the service delay to train passengers via the public address system.
  • OCC Coordination: Take further action strictly as per the directives of the OCC/TC while keeping passengers regularly informed of the unfolding situation.

Duties of the Traffic Controller (TC)

  • Emergency Notification: Inform all concerned personnel and departments as per the standard OCC communication list.
  • System-Wide Announcements: Ensure regular announcements are made to passengers on trains and at stations regarding likely delays and ongoing evacuation procedures until the position becomes clear.
  • Service Resumption: Train movement shall only be resumed after confirming that the running of trains through the affected station is completely safe.

STAMPEDE

Subtitle: Electrical Safety and Power Isolation Protocols

Duties of the Traction Power Controller (CTPC/TPC)

  • Power De-energization: Normally, no power supply switching OFF decision shall be taken independently by the CTPC/TPC; however, if instructed by the Traffic Controller, the OHE/33kV power supply shall be switched OFF on the respective lines.
  • Field Communication & Restoration: Convey the emergency message to senior officers and field staff as per the situation. If needed, instruct OHE staff to reach the station to provide discharge rods, but only after receiving clearance from the CC/TC.
  • Information Logging: Collect and report vital information to senior officers, including:
    • Exact place of the stampede.
    • Extent of damage caused.
    • Number and details of casualties, if any.
    • Current status of the incident and relief operations.

TERRORIST ATTACK

Subtitle: Threat Scenarios and Chemical/Biological/Radioactive (CBR) Protocols

System Overview & Attack Scenarios

  • Potential Attack Scenarios: Terrorist attacks on the Metro network can occur in multiple forms, including:
    • Armed attack, intrusion, indiscriminate firing, or Fidayeen attack on station concourses, platforms, vital installations, or Operations Control Centers (OCC).
    • Armed attacks targeting VVIPs/VIPs visiting metro stations.
    • Intrusion of explosive-laden vehicles into receiving sub-stations and train depots forcibly or by deceit.
    • Human bombs, suicide bombers, or Fidayeen attackers sneaking into stations as passengers, visitors, or impersonating NMRC employees, police, or army personnel.
    • Use of adjacent buildings in close proximity to throw grenades onto stations or trains.

TERRORIST ATTACK

Subtitle: Emergency Response at Station Premises

Duties of the Station Manager / Station Controller (SM/SC)

  • Site Assessment & OCC Alert: Visit the affected spot to assess the impact on human life and train services, and immediately inform the OCC regarding incident details, time of occurrence, extent of injuries, and presence of smoke or fire.
  • Emergency Services Coordination: Assess the need for medical assistance and fire services, indicate the specific gate through which emergency teams should enter, and depute uniformed staff to receive and guide them.
  • Evacuation & PA Announcements: Sound the station hooter and get the premises vacated of all passengers immediately. Depute staff to announce at regular intervals via the PA system explaining what has happened and guiding passengers to exit without panic.
  • Medical Relief & Security: Mobilize resources to render first aid and evacuate the injured. With the help of security staff, detain any suspicious persons for interrogation.
  • Witness Documentation: Record statements, names, and addresses of passengers found near the affected area who have first-hand knowledge of the occurrence.

TERRORIST ATTACK

Subtitle: System-Wide Control Room Containment Protocols

Duties of the Traffic Controller (TC) / Chief Controller (CC)

  • Agency Notification & Mobilization: Inform all concerned personnel as per the OCC communication list and immediately mobilize medical assistance and fire services to the spot.
  • Network Clearance Protocol: Upon receipt of information regarding bomb explosions, fire, poisonous gas/chemical release, or any act endangering safety, take prompt action to clear the entire metro network:
    • Instruct Train Operators (TOs) of stationary trains to discharge passengers and request them to leave the station quickly.
    • Instruct TOs of running trains to stop at the first station, discharge all passengers, and take a safe position 50 meters away from the train.
    • Instruct Station Managers/Controllers to evacuate premises without delay and immediately stop the sale of tickets.
  • System Closure & Resumption: Ensure regular announcements are made until all passengers are evacuated. The entire Metro network shall remain closed until rescue and search operations are completed; revenue operations shall only resume after ensuring the system is fully safe and secure.

TERRORIST ATTACK

Subtitle: Mid-Section Attacks Affecting the Right of Way

Duties of the Train Operator (TO)

  • Immediate Reporting & Transit: On noticing any unusual occurrence on track or installations, report immediately to the OCC. If train movement is not obstructed, continue journey to the next station at reduced speed while informing OCC.
  • Immediate Halt & Protection: If train running is unsafe, stop the train forthwith, inform the OCC for further directions, and announce the delay to passengers. If the adjacent track is damaged or shows abnormalities, immediately report to the TC and protect the track as per prescribed procedures.
  • Passenger Information: Take further action as directed by the OCC while keeping passengers regularly informed of the situation.

Duties of the Operational Control Centre (OCC)

  • Traffic Regulation: Take immediate action to regulate trains and prevent them from running over the affected section.
  • System Evacuation & Communication: Take prompt action to clear the entire metro network of all passengers. Inform all concerned departments as per the communication list and make system-wide announcements to keep customers fully informed.

Chemical & Gas Release

Detection & Symptoms

  • Unusual smell.
  • Breathing problems, including choking or fainting.
  • Severe eye or skin irritation.
  • Vomiting.

Immediate Response Actions

  • The train has to be completely vacated.
  • To prevent further spreading of gas in the platform area, train doors will be closed.
  • The incident will be dealt with by Police, Medical, and metro authorities.

SABOTAGE

Definition & Potential Scenarios

  • Definition: Sabotage is a treacherous action to defeat or hinder a cause, endeavor, or deliberate subversion.
  • Targeting Vital Installations: Sabotage can occur at metro stations (e.g., Station Control Room (SCR), Auxiliary Sub Stations).
  • System-Wide Targets: Threats may target the Operations Control Center (OCC), Security Control Room, Receiving Sub Stations, or trains parked in depots.
  • Insider Threats: Influencing NMRC, housekeeping, or security staff for monetary or other reasons to extract vital information to sabotage the system.
  • Cyber Threats: Cyber attacks targeting the computer-based signal system.

SABOTAGE

Subtitle: Emergency Response of Field and Station Personnel

Duties of the Train Operator (TO)

  • Immediate Reporting: In the event of sabotage taking place involving their train, the TO shall immediately inform the OCC, giving as many details as possible.
  • Track Protection: In case the adjacent track is infringed, the TO shall request the Traffic Controller (TC) to protect the adjacent track to avoid multiple accidents as per prescribed procedures.
  • Passenger Communication: Inform passengers about the incident, advising them about the rescue and relief arrangements being made.
  • Injury Assessment: Assess the situation particularly in respect of passenger injury and again inform the OCC with as much detail as available, seeking medical and other assistance as required.

Duties of the Station Controller (SC)

  • OCC Alert & Details: Inform the OCC immediately, giving as many details as possible regarding the sabotage incident.
  • Rescue & First Aid Mobilization: Mobilize staff for the rapid evacuation of passengers, rendering of first aid to the injured, and arranging their hospitalization as required.
  • Crowd Communication: Inform passengers waiting at the station of the likely service delay.

SABOTAGE

Subtitle: Control Room Containment and Electrical Safety Protocols

Duties of the Traffic Controller (TC) / Chief Controller (CC)

  • Emergency Notification: Immediately on receipt of information about sabotage, the TC/CC shall inform all concerned personnel as per the standard OCC communication list.
  • Secondary Disaster Response: If the sabotage has resulted in fire, derailment, or a train accident, the NMRC response will be executed as described for those specific scenarios.
  • Service Regulation & Resumption: Train movement shall only be resumed after confirming that running trains to the affected station is completely safe. Until the position becomes clear, regular announcements must be made to passengers on trains and at stations regarding likely delays and evacuation procedures.

Duties of the Traction Power Controller (CTPC/TPC)

  • Power De-energization: Normally, no power supply switching OFF decision shall be taken independently by the CTPC/TPC; however, if instructed by the Traffic Controller, the OHE/33kV power supply shall be switched OFF on the respective lines.
  • Field Communication & Restoration: Convey the emergency message to senior officers and field staff as per the situation. If needed, instruct OHE staff to reach the station to provide discharge rods, but only after receiving clearance from the CC/TC.
  • Information Logging: On receipt of information, collect and report vital details to senior officers, including the exact place of sabotage, extent of damage, services affected, modus operandi used, and latest status.

EARTHQUAKE

Subtitle: Seismic Risk Overview and System Design Safety Features

System Overview & Infrastructure Safety

  • Seismic Zone & History: Delhi is located in seismic zone (4) and has a documented history of seismic tremors.
  • Structural Resilience: The NMRC network has been specifically designed to withstand earthquakes of an intensity of 7 on the Richter scale.
  • Sensor Network: Dedicated earthquake sensors have been installed at Patel Chowk and Vidhan Sabha stations of Line-2 to detect tremors.
  • Initial Power Action: Upon tremor detection, the Traction Power Controller (TPC) will switch off the Traction Power Supply in a manner that does not shut down station supplies, while immediately informing the Traffic Controller.

Subtitle: Emergency Response of Onboard Operating Staff

Duties of the Train Operator (TO)

  • Immediate Halt & Alert: In the event of a significant earthquake, the TO—upon experiencing the impact or being informed by passengers or the OCC—must bring the train to a stop immediately and inform the OCC of the train's exact location.
  • Station Protocol: If the train is at a station, the TO will not move the train, will inform the OCC, and will advise passengers to remain inside the train. Once OCC instructions are received confirming the earthquake has subsided, trains waiting at stations will detrain passengers.
  • Mid-Section Protocol & Visual Check: For trains held up between stations, TOs must visually check the track. If the track is unaffected and there are no visible obstructions, after informing the OCC/TC, the train can be moved in Restricted Manual (RM) mode at walking speed up to the next station where passengers shall be detrained.
  • Passenger Communication: The TO will keep passengers continuously informed of the problem and request them to maintain calm.

Subtitle: Control Room Regulation and Service Resumption Protocols

Duties of the Traffic Controller (TC)

  • System-Wide Halt: The TC shall instruct all Train Operators to stop their trains immediately and report their positions.
  • Mid-Section Guidance: For trains held up between stations, the OCC/TC will instruct TOs to visually check the track. If clear of visible obstructions, the TC authorizes moving the train in Restricted Manual (RM) mode at walking speed to the next station to detrain passengers.
  • Evacuation Contingency: In case of any doubt regarding track safety, the OCC will arrange for passenger evacuation on the right of way as per established procedure and mobilize resources for rescue and relief work.
  • Service Resumption: Normal operation of revenue trains shall only be resumed after the Track and Structures department issues a certificate of fitness for normal operations following a detailed physical inspection.
  • Passenger Information: The OCC will ensure that passengers are continuously informed about the unfolding situation and the likely time for the commencement of train services.

Subtitle: Station Premises Inspection and Electrical Safety Protocols

Duties of the Station Manager / Station Controller (SM/SC)

  • Premises Inspection: Inspect all station premises immediately; if there is any abnormality, inform the OCC and call other emergency services for help.
  • Relief Mobilization: Mobilize station resources for rescue and relief work while continuously seeking instructions from the OCC.
  • Crowd Communication: Continuously inform passengers awaiting at the station regarding the situation.

Duties of the Traction Power Controller (CTPC/TPC)

  • Power De-energization: On getting information about an earthquake, power supply should be switched OFF immediately and advised accordingly. Only after getting explicit clearance from the TC can the OHE be charged again.
  • Field Communication & Discharge Rods: Convey the message to senior officers and field staff as per the situation. If the need arises, instruct OHE staff to reach the station to provide discharge rods, but strictly only after getting clearance from the CC/TC.
  • Information Logging: Try to extract vital information and keep senior officers reported regarding: the affected area of the disaster, extent of damage, services affected, and current status.

Flooding & Wind Speed Restrictions

Track Flooding Protocols

  • Any staff observing water accumulation must report exact location and water level relative to rails to TC.
  • Water Below Rail Fastenings: Train speed is restricted to a maximum of 25 km/h through the section.
  • Water Above Rail Fastenings: Passenger services are suspended; movement permitted only under special instructions.
